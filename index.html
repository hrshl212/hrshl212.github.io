GPU & HPC engineering · FlowFPM++

Porting a CPU research code to GPUs, guided by profiling.

I took a validated MPI post-processor for flow simulations and rebuilt its compute core to run on NVIDIA GPUs with MPI + OpenACC. The CPU version stayed the numerical reference throughout: every GPU kernel was checked field by field against it, then profiled and optimized one measured change at a time. The core workload is a large 3-D Poisson-type solve on stretched grids with immersed solid bodies, followed by volume integrals over millions of cells.

117×Faster solver sweep than one CPU core (4.2M cells)
6.9×Faster time-to-solution than 16 MPI ranks (16.8M cells)
~80%Of A100 peak memory bandwidth sustained
10⁻¹⁷CPU/GPU agreement on an 87M-cell production frame

On one CPU core the 4.2M-cell solve takes 57.6 s (46× slower than the GPU). Small grids that fit in the CPU caches show little gain, and the repository reports those results too.

Profile, change, measure

Each change was driven by a profile (NVHPC kernel timing and Nsight Systems) and measured against the previous build on the same node.

  • Keep data resident on the GPUProfiling showed 11 field uploads and 5 downloads around a 0.7 ms kernel; a frame-level data region removed them.
    5× faster terms
  • Remove a copy kernelPing-pong between two padded buffers instead of copying back after every sweep.
    1.5× per sweep
  • Build solver setup on the deviceCoefficients and preconditioner built in GPU kernels, not uploaded from the host.
    no per-solve uploads
  • Change the algorithmReplaced Jacobi iteration (900k sweeps) with preconditioned conjugate gradients, with GPU reductions and breakdown guards.
    87× faster solve
  • Fix GPU-only correctness bugsFound residual drift and a stop condition the compiled GPU loop did not see; both fixed and covered by tests.
    regression-tested

GPU & HPC engineering · C90 CFD-DEM

Scaling a coupled fluid-particle solver to 180 million particles on one H100.

At Purdue I extended MFiX-Exa (AMReX, CUDA) with a 90-degree rotationally periodic sector for a syringe-injection CFD-DEM problem, then made it fast on NVIDIA H100s. Every optimization was chosen from a profile, tested as a matched A/B against the previous build, and accepted only if pressure, particle state, and particle count stayed within an explicit accuracy budget.

180MParticles on a single 80 GB H100 at 6.0 s per step
203×Fewer neighbor candidates; fixed a 32-bit CSR overflow crash
5.1×Faster step from a profile-guided neighbor-list fix (20M particles)
2.04×Faster pressure solve with a best-iterate Krylov bottom solver

Profile, change, measure

Stage timers, nested GPU timers, and Nsight Compute picked every target. Each gain below is from an on/off A/B on the same H100.

  • Diagnose a crash at its rootA 64-bit pre-scan audit showed the 80M neighbor list needed 7.3× the 32-bit CSR limit (~125 GB). Size-aware binning fixed the algorithm instead of widening the offsets.
    203× fewer entries
  • Sort particles spatiallyNsight Compute showed the pair kernel was latency-bound on irregular gathers, with too little occupancy to hide them. Periodic spatial sorting restored locality.
    52.8% faster DEM substep
  • Cache a recursive multigrid operatorAssembled the coarse Galerkin operator once as a width-48 ELLPACK sparse matrix on the GPU, matching the recursive operator to 6×10⁻¹⁵.
    30.1% faster step
  • Remove device-host synchronizationGuarded no-sync execution through the one-rank multigrid hierarchy.
    33.2% faster step
  • Exploit symmetryA quarter-cylinder sector on the rotational quotient space, validated against the full cylinder.
    1.28× at 0.11% error

Research philosophy

From numerical method to physical insight.

My work connects three layers that are often treated separately: faithful mathematical representation, high-performance simulation, and interpretable scientific discovery.

01

Represent the physics

Design sharp-interface, immersed-boundary, level-set, VOF, and CFD-DEM formulations for moving interfaces, phase change, and interacting solids.

02

Compute at scale

Build and parallelize research solvers in C/C++, CUDA, MPI, OpenACC, and finite-element ecosystems for high-throughput studies.

03

Explain and control

Use force partitioning, reduced models, scaling laws, and scientific machine learning to identify mechanisms and guide design.

Ph.D. research · IIT Bombay

Controlling nucleate boiling with moving surfaces.

My doctoral work developed a sharp-interface dual-grid level-set and immersed-boundary solver for multiphase flow with phase change and moving solids. The simulations below show how surface motion and immersed structures reshape bubble departure, synchronization, and heat transfer.

One solver, three scientific outcomes.

The work established lock-on mechanisms in forced boiling, extended the method to 3D multi-site configurations, enabled a dual-grid deep-reinforcement-learning framework, and contributed to an issued patent for sustaining nucleate boiling in zero gravity.

Rotational quotient-space formulation

A validated 90° cyclic sector for coupled CFD-DEM

The sector framework maps scalars across radial faces, rotates vectors by 90°, wraps particles and their contact images, conserves fluxes and particle-to-fluid sources, and makes the pressure projection act on the rotational quotient space.
